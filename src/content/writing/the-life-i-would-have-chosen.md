---
title: The Life I Would Have Chosen
description: On Alan Watts, planning, and choosing a life you can't predict
date: 2026-11-11
---

Imagine you could dream any life you want.

And every night, you get to dream an entire lifetime. Seventy-five years where you can experience absolutely anything you wish for. You can be anyone, go anywhere, have everything you've ever wanted. And you get total control over everything that happens in every dream.

You'd probably start by living out all the things you've _dreamed_ of in your current life. All the money in the world. Meeting the love of your life. Visiting every place you've always wanted to see. No responsibilities, and everything going exactly as planned. Straight dopamine, for seventy-five years.

But how many nights can you actually keep doing that before you get bored?

Eventually, you'd probably want something unexpected to happen. Maybe you'd throw in a bunch of challenges, or let things go wrong every now and then, just to keep it interesting. There's still always a happy ending, though. Until even that gets boring, and you start giving up a little more control each night.

Then one night, you might choose a dream where you have no idea who you're going to be born as, what's going to happen, or whether there's a happy ending at all. Take it one step further and you might even forget you were dreaming in the first place.

This is a thought experiment from Alan Watts's _The Dream of Life_, and I've been thinking about it a lot lately.

The part that's stuck with me most is the possibility that, given the choice to live any life imaginable, I might eventually pick the exact life I'm living right now.

---

And yet I spend a lot of energy trying to make the future more predictable.

I think about my career, my finances, where I want to live, and what I want my life to look like in five years or ten years. Or even twenty years. I try to plan everything because I want things to work out.

But suppose someone offered me a way to know exactly what my life is going to look like ten years from now.

Would I take it?

My first instinct is of course yes. I want to know if everything turns out okay. But the more I think about it, the less appealing it sounds.

Imagine knowing exactly who you'd meet, what your best moments are, which mistakes you'd make, and how every major decision would turn out. You would never have to wonder about anything again.

I think I'd hate that.

---

I don't actually believe that I chose my life before I was born. At least, I don't think I have a strong enough reason to.

But what if I pretended, just for a moment?

That somewhere, after living through every perfect life, I decided I wanted to try something different. Something where I can't control everything, where I wanted to figure things out as I went along.

And somehow, this is what I ended up choosing.

It makes ordinary events feel a little more interesting. The city I live in, the people I spend time with, the things I worry about that won't matter in a few years.

In fact, a lot of what's happened to me traces back to small accidents. Like my first job out of college, or who I was roomed with freshman year.

I wonder what my younger self would think if I showed him what life looks like today. Probably that a lot of things turned out better than expected. Some things differently. And that I still have no idea what I'm doing half the time.

I'll probably keep making five-year plans anyway and forget I wrote any of this. But if I did pick this life, I picked it knowing I'd be the kind of person who makes them.
